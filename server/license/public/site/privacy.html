Privacy Policy — Pulse Player

Last updated: November 4, 2026

This is the English version of the Portuguese privacy policy. If the two differ, the Portuguese version prevails. 한국어

What the app is

Pulse Player is a generic media player. It does not provide, host, endorse or sell any content. The user enters the addresses (server or playlist) from which they want to play media and is solely responsible for them.

What the app does not do

  • It does not send personal, usage or diagnostic data to the developer or to third parties, except what the user chooses to send from the phone (optional item in the table above), which travels encrypted and only their TV can open, and the license data described in the table (device identifier, playlist domain and dates of use), which is used only for the trial, activation and billing of partner servers and is never sold.
  • It does not use advertising, trackers, usage analytics or third-party cookies.
  • It does not access contacts, location, camera or microphone. (The phone only reads the QR code with the device's own camera app; Pulse Player does not ask for camera access.)

Network connections

The app only connects to the addresses the user registered (API, playlists, video streams and the cover/logo images those servers point to). These servers belong to third parties and have their own policies; the app does not control what they record.

If the user chooses "Experimentar com vídeos de demonstração" (Try with demo videos) on the sign-in screen, the app downloads the demo playlist from the address published by the developer and plays free-to-use videos and public test streams (Blender Foundation via archive.org, Mux and Apple). No user data is sent in this case.

Add a playlist from the phone (optional)

When the app version includes this option (the sign-in screen shows "Use o seu celular" / Use your phone), the app talks to the developer's pairing service at https://par.pulseplayer.com.br:

  1. The TV requests a short code and sends the service a public key generated on the spot. The private key stays only in the TV's memory and never leaves it.
  2. The user opens the service's page on the phone (through the QR code or by typing the short address), pastes the playlist link and taps send. The page encrypts the playlist on the phone itself with the TV's key (end-to-end encryption); the service receives only the encrypted text.
  3. The TV collects the encrypted text, opens it with the private key and shows the received server for the user to confirm before adding it. The service deletes the text the moment it is read; if nobody reads it, it disappears on its own within 10 minutes at most (60 seconds after it was sent).

The service runs on Cloudflare's infrastructure (Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects), which processes network requests (seeing the IP address of whoever connects) under the provider's own privacy policy. Worker observability and logs are explicitly turned off in the configuration, so no request body, key or secret is recorded. The service cannot read the playlist, does not write its content to disk or to a database, and does not use cookies, ads or trackers; the phone page loads no third-party resources. The service sees the network address (IP) of whoever connects only to prevent abuse, through counters kept in volatile memory for up to 1 minute.

Users who do not use this option send nothing to the service: the TV contacts it only while the "Use o seu celular" screen is open.

Cast to a TV (optional, phones only)

In the Android phone and tablet app, the player shows "Transmitir" (Cast) when the device has Google Play Services. When the user picks a TV with Google Cast (Chromecast or a compatible TV), the app sends the video address (which includes the playlist's user name and password) and the title to that TV over the local network. The TV opens Google's standard receiver (Default Media Receiver) and fetches the video directly from the user's server; the app does not route the video through any developer server. Discovering TVs and connecting to them is done by the device's Google Play Services, under Google's privacy policy. While casting is active, other devices on the same network may see what is playing on the TV. Users who do not tap "Transmitir" send nothing to any TV.

Control and deletion

  • Remove a playlist: Dashboard → Listas (Playlists) → trash icon. Deletes the saved playlist and the encrypted password.
  • Sign out: Dashboard → Listas → "Sair da conta atual" (Sign out). Ends the session and deletes the cached catalog; saved playlists stay on the device until removed with the trash icon.
  • Delete everything: clear the app's data in the device settings or uninstall the app.
  • Pairing from the phone: there is nothing to delete in the service after use (it has already deleted it). To cancel earlier, just close the screen or create a new code on the TV: the old code stops working within 10 minutes at most.

Children

The app is not directed at children and has no content of its own: what appears depends on the playlists the user adds. To protect children, the app has parental control: categories with adult-content names start locked (they open only with a PIN, created on first use and changeable later), and any category can be locked or hidden per profile ("Controle parental" button in the Movies, Series and Channels tabs). Items from locked or hidden categories also do not appear on the Home screen or in search. Parents and guardians should create the PIN before handing over the device and supervise its use.
